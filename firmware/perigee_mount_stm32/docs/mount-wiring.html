Mount wiring, axis limits and cable-wrap safety

A Nucleo-F401RE driving two goBILDA Stingray servo gearboxes, both open loop: no encoder on either axis, and the pulse width is the position. Every pin, pulse and limit on this page was read back out of src/hw.rs, src/limits.rs, src/main.rs and control.toml rather than from notes — the firmware is the source of truth.

The Uno's D9 / D10 assignment carried straight over to the Nucleo's Arduino header, which is why the numbers look familiar. The silicon behind them is different: hardware PWM out of TIM3 and TIM4 in 0.3125 µs steps, not the Servo library's software timing.

Timer setup

Both timers run from the internal 16 MHz oscillator (HSI, untouched, so every bus is at 16 MHz) with PSC = 4, giving a 3.2 MHz tick, and ARR = 63999 for a 20 ms frame — 50 Hz, as the gearboxes require. One tick of pulse width is therefore 0.3125 µs, which is 0.070° on azimuth and 0.031° on elevation. Preload is on for both channels, so a new width always starts on a frame boundary and a pulse is never truncated mid-cycle.

The rest of the peripheral set

  • USART2 into DMA. Received bytes land in a 1024-byte circular buffer on DMA1 stream 5, channel 4, so a slow reply never drops an incoming command. A burst bigger than the buffer (a long script pasted in one go) is reported, ERR receive overrun: input lost, resend, instead of losing commands silently.
  • Independent watchdog, about 1 s. LSI 32 kHz with PR = 4 and RLR = 500. If the main loop stalls, the chip resets and the servos go limp. It is frozen while a debugger has the core halted.
  • No ADC and no I2C. Not merely unused: unclocked and unconfigured. There is no encoder on either axis.
PA0 and PA1 are deliberately empty

These gearboxes are positional servos with a 3-position TJC8 connector (signal, V+, GND) and a 5 kΩ pot internal to the servo's own control loop. There is no fourth wire and no feedback output. The old Arduino sketch and the first STM32 build read A0 / A1 as “feedback wires”; those reads returned floating-pin noise, and acting on them would have been worse than reading nothing.

Power

One rail for muscle, one for the board, one ground

Two rules, each of which has a way of destroying something:

  1. Each servo's red and black go straight to the rail. Not through the Nucleo, not daisy-chained through each other. The board's regulator cannot pass amps; routing servo current through it resets or destroys the board.
  2. Common ground is mandatory, at one star point. Rail negative, a Nucleo GND pin and both servo blacks all land on the same node. Without the tie the servos have no reference for the pulse and behave randomly or not at all.

The 10 A figure is 2 × 3.0 A stall at 7.4 V, times 1.7 for brushed-DC reversal transients and inrush. Typical tracking draw is well under 1 A total; the rating exists for the stall and reversal case, not for normal operation. 3.3 V logic is in spec, since the published pulse amplitude is 3–5 V. If a gearbox ignores the signal over 300 mm of 22 AWG lead, a 74AHCT125 buffer on 5 V fixes it; that is a fallback, not a starting requirement.

Limits

Why azimuth cannot tangle the cable

Two frames, and the difference matters. A gearbox angle runs 0 to the mechanical travel, measured from the low end stop. A mount angle is what the protocol and the host talk in, and mount = gearbox − offset. The offset is therefore both the frame tie and the mechanical margin at the low end: with AZ_CAL.offset = 8.0, mount azimuth 0 sits 8° off the hard stop. Eight rather than a tighter two, because goBILDA publish two slopes that disagree by 2 %, and only with 8° does the window stay clear of both stops under either one. Elevation's offset = 5.0 is the built geometry, and its lower limit of −2° rather than −5° is what buys margin at the bottom stop — at −5° the gearbox would sit at exactly 0°, hard against the stop, with a 9 kg dish on the arm.

Above that sits an independent backstop. gear_margin = 2.0 keeps 2° of gearbox travel clear of each mechanical end whatever the soft limits say, so a mis-edited limit_hi still cannot reach a stop. There is a host test for exactly that case.

Azimuth is an absolute, continuous, unwrapped angle: 0 at one mechanical end of the Stingray-4's 450° of travel, rising monotonically to 450 at the other. There is no modular arithmetic on this axis anywhere, in the firmware or on the host, so a move from A to B always traverses [min(A,B), max(A,B)] and nothing else. A command can never be satisfied “the short way round” through the cable wrap.

Two independent layers

Layer 1 · host, plans

perigee-control/src/mount.rs decides which wrap to use. nearest_allowed_az is the point rule; solve_path picks a single wrap for a whole predicted pass. A bug here costs a pass.

Layer 2 · firmware, enforces

firmware/…/src/limits.rs enforces the window regardless. Every pulse goes through AxisCal::pulse_of, clamped into the mount window and then into the servo's pulse range. A bug on the host cannot reach the cable.

The 399 → 401 case, concretely

  1. The azimuth axis is at 399°. The next pointing wants the direction that 401° would reach — two degrees away, and straight through the cable.
  2. The host asks nearest_allowed_az. 401 is outside the 0 … 400 window, so it is not a candidate at all. Its whole-turn equivalent 41° is inside, and it is the only candidate.
  3. The answer is 41, so the mount unwinds 358° the other way rather than tangling two.
  4. Had the host sent 401 anyway, the firmware would reply ERR out of window, clamped to 400.00 and stop at the limit. It does not reason about wraps: it refuses and clamps. 401 becomes 400, never 41.

Wrap chosen once per pass, not per sample

solve_path unwraps a whole predicted track into one continuous azimuth run, then shifts it by whole turns to find an offset where the entire pass fits inside the window. Where several offsets fit, the one whose start is nearest the current axis position wins (shortest slew to the AOS point), and margin to the limits breaks remaining ties. Where no offset fits, because the pass runs across the seam at the window's ends, one mid-pass unwind is scheduled at the split point that loses the fewest samples (the dish runs the long way round, about 16 s off the satellite at 20 deg/s) and recorded so the tracker logs it instead of the operator discovering it. In a count of 908 real passes that was 3–6 % of them.

Because the window spans more than a full turn, 40° of bearings are reachable at two axis positions 360° apart; choosing between them is the whole game. There is no flip-over (removed 2026-10-01 for simplicity): elevation stops a degree past vertical, so each sky direction has one pose. A pass near the zenith swings the azimuth quickly at the top and may lag for a few seconds there, which costs little: near the zenith an azimuth error barely moves the beam.

Two places, edited together

The limit numbers live in two places and must be edited together: az_limit_lo_deg / az_limit_hi_deg / el_min_deg / el_max_deg on the host, and AZ_CAL / EL_CAL in limits.rs. As of this page they agree: control.toml carries 0 / 400 and −2 / 91, and its park pose (200, 45) matches the firmware's PARK. The host narrows anything wider into the firmware window on load, so an edit to control.toml alone can only tighten it.

On boot: READY PERIGEE-MOUNT fw4-stm32 PROTO T4, then either POS <az> <el> restored, holding or POS UNKNOWN.

No telemetry field is a measurement

tgt is the requested target after the window clamp, cmd the slew-limited command that is the pulse on the wire, and set that command lagged by a pessimistic servo speed model. Neither axis has an encoder, so fields 7–8 are always NA -1; they stay only so the line shape does not change. flags: K position known, M moving, A / E azimuth / elevation target clipped at the limit, - for none.

Position across a reset, and not across a power cycle

The last position is kept in a .uninit region of SRAM that cortex-m-rt's startup code does not clear, behind a magic word (0x5047_4D04) and a rotated-XOR checksum that catches a half-written record, uninitialised RAM that happens to hold the magic, and NaN or infinite angles.

  • A system reset keeps it. The 1 s watchdog, the reset button, a probe-rs run — SRAM survives all three, so the firmware restores the position and immediately holds the same pulses instead of slewing blind or jumping on the first command.
  • A power cycle loses it. SRAM is gone, the checksum fails, the board stays limp and reports POS UNKNOWN. Every move is refused (ERR position unknown) until ZERO az el arrives.

Surviving a power cycle would need the RTC backup registers and a VBAT cell. That is a deliberate next step, not something this build claims.

The limit of what software can prove

Every number in the pulse map is arithmetic on bench constants that have not been measured yet. The tests show the arithmetic is self-consistent and that no path through the code can exceed its own window. They cannot show the window is in the right place on real hardware. That is what the checklist below is for.

Bench checklist

Unverified until the hardware is in front of you

  1. Measure each axis's slope inside the window.

    RAW moves to the angle its pulse width means, ramped like GO and clamped into the soft window (RAW AZ 500 ends at 535.56 µs), so it cannot reach a stop and is not meant to. Step RAW AZ from 600 to 2250 and RAW EL from 600 to 1400, read the angle at each step, and fit us_per_deg. Full procedure: docs/calibration.md §6.3.

  2. Check the azimuth zero offset.

    AZ_CAL.offset = 8.0 assumes mount azimuth 0 sits 8° off the low stop. Drive GO 0 45 and confirm there is still a visible gap to the stop. The gap you measure goes into pulse_at_zero (docs/calibration.md §6.4); the offset itself stays 8.

  3. Check the elevation offset.

    EL_CAL.offset = 5.0 is the built geometry. Drive GO 200 0 and confirm the boresight is level. If it is not, both elevation limits move with the correction.

  4. Re-zero after every power cycle.

    A cold boot reports POS UNKNOWN and stays limp. Send ZERO az el with where the axes physically are: until then every motion command is refused (ERR position unknown), so nothing slews from a guessed origin. ZERO is accepted only while the servos are limp; to re-declare a board that is holding, send OFF first. A reset button press or watchdog bite does not need this; SRAM holds the position through both.

  5. Confirm 3.3 V signalling over the real lead length.

    Published pulse amplitude is 3–5 V, so 3.3 V is in spec, but 300 mm of 22 AWG has not been tried. If a gearbox ignores the signal, a 74AHCT125 buffer on 5 V is the fix.

  6. Sky calibration, afterwards.

    The sky tie lives on the host, in perigee-control's calibration.toml, not in the firmware: az_center_bearing_deg ties mount azimuth to a true bearing, and an optional elevation correction ties mount elevation to the real one. Point the dish at something whose direction you know and type /here AZ EL in the console (or /bearing DEG); /here clear forgets it. Until then the frame is a nominal 217.5 (the middle of the 120–315 dish sector) and a real mount will not track.
